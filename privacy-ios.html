Hidden Phrase: Clover’s Corner for iPhone · Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 29, 2026

Hidden Phrase: Clover’s Corner ("the app", shown on your Home Screen as "Hidden Phrase") is a word puzzle game for iPhone made by Nathanael Berkeley ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the app uses, why, and the choices you have.

In short

  • You don’t need an account. The app never asks for your name, email address, contacts, photos or precise location.
  • Your puzzle progress is saved on your iPhone. It is not sent to us.
  • The app offers optional rewarded ads from Google AdMob. The app asks Google to show only non-personalized ads. Google’s ad software still collects some device information to show, measure and protect those ads, as described below.
  • From version 1.3, the app asks once for Apple’s permission to track, so we can measure which of our own Facebook and Instagram ads bring new players. Only if you allow tracking does the app send Meta app-activity information with your device’s Advertising Identifier. If you don’t allow it, the app doesn’t start Meta’s software and nothing is sent to Meta.
  • The game, hints, rewarded ads, room decorations and widgets work the same whatever you choose.

Information saved on your iPhone

The app saves your puzzle progress (boards, completed puzzles, the puzzle to continue), hints, earned and placed room decorations, and your settings (key-tap vibration, reduced celebration motion) in a file inside the app’s own storage on your iPhone. It also remembers which rewarded-ad letter is owed to which puzzle until the reward is delivered.

  • This information is not sent to us, and we have no server that receives it.
  • The progress file is excluded from iCloud Backup and from backups made with a computer, so it is not restored to a new or reset iPhone.
  • Deleting the app deletes this information, and we cannot recover it. (If iOS offloads the app to save space, iOS keeps the app’s data until you reinstall or delete it.)

Google’s software also stores some information in the app’s storage on your iPhone, including your ad privacy choices, so it can apply them each time the app starts. If you have allowed tracking, Meta’s software also stores its settings for the app and a random app-installation ID that it creates. Unlike the progress file, this information can be included in your iPhone’s backups.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app uses the Google Mobile Ads SDK, provided by Google, to offer rewarded ads: you can choose to watch an ad to reveal one letter. There are no banner ads and no ads that interrupt play. To have an ad ready when you choose one, the app loads ads in the background once Google’s consent tool indicates that ad requests are permitted. This means Google’s SDK may collect data even if you never watch an ad.

Ads are selected through Google’s ad platform and may come from many different advertisers. The only other advertising software in the app is Meta’s, used to measure our own ads (see the next section); it shows no ads.

Non-personalized ads only. For every ad request, the app tells Google to turn off ads personalization, and it turns off Google’s publisher first-party ID (an identifier Google can otherwise assign to personalize ads within our apps). Google describes non-personalized ads as ads that are not based on your past behavior; they use contextual information such as general (for example city-level) location and the app you are using. Google says non-personalized ads still use identifiers for frequency capping (limiting how often you see the same ad) and aggregated ad reporting.

Advertising Identifier. iOS gives apps your device’s Advertising Identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking (see the next section). If you allow it, Google’s software in the app can also read the IDFA. The app still asks Google for non-personalized ads, so the IDFA is not used to choose ads based on your behavior; Google says such identifiers are used for frequency capping and aggregated ad reporting. If you don’t allow tracking, iOS gives neither the app nor Google the IDFA.

According to the privacy disclosures Google includes with its iOS software, the Google Mobile Ads SDK can collect:

  • Coarse location, such as a general location estimated from your IP address.
  • Device identifiers, including the IDFA if you allow tracking, and identifiers iOS makes available to apps without tracking permission.
  • Advertising data, such as the ads you are shown.
  • Product interaction, such as app launches, taps and ad views.
  • Performance, crash and other diagnostic data about the app and the SDK.

Google’s consent tool (User Messaging Platform) uses coarse location, performance data and product interaction to work out which privacy choices apply to you and to show them.

Google uses this information to show and measure ads, for analytics, and for fraud prevention and security, as described in its own policies. Data sent by Google’s software is encrypted in transit. We use AdMob reporting to understand overall advertising performance. We do not receive identifiers for individual users through the game, and we do not link advertising reports to your support emails or puzzle progress.

The app also lists advertising networks for Apple’s SKAdNetwork, Apple’s privacy-preserving way to report whether an ad led to an app install without identifying you. On its first launch the app registers with this system, so an ad network that showed you an ad for Hidden Phrase can receive Apple’s anonymous install report. iOS sends that report, not the app; it doesn’t need tracking permission and contains no identifier for you.

Google’s handling of this data is described in:

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services

Measuring our ads on Facebook and Instagram (Meta)

We advertise Hidden Phrase on Facebook and Instagram. To learn which of those ads lead to new players, the app includes Meta’s software development kit (the Facebook SDK’s core part only; there is no Facebook login and Meta shows no ads in the app).

Apple’s tracking permission. After Google’s privacy message (if one is shown), iOS asks once whether to allow Hidden Phrase to track your activity across other companies’ apps and websites. Nothing in the game depends on your answer.

  • If you allow tracking, Meta’s software sends Meta app events, such as the app being opened, together with your device’s Advertising Identifier, a random app-installation ID created by Meta’s software, and device information (such as device model, iOS version, language, time zone, screen size and app version). Meta also receives your IP address, which it can use to estimate a general location. Meta uses this to attribute installs and app use to our ads and to report ad results to us, and, under its own terms, may combine it with information from other companies’ apps and websites to measure advertising.
  • If you don’t allow tracking, haven’t answered yet, or tracking is restricted on your iPhone, the app doesn’t start Meta’s software at all, so nothing is sent to Meta.

Always off: the app turns off Meta’s automatic collection of on-screen information and its product-improvement diagnostics, and logs no purchases or other in-app actions of its own; the only events are Meta’s automatic ones: the app being installed and opened, your tracking-permission status, and technical events about the SDK’s own settings.

Limited Data Use. The app always tells Meta to apply Limited Data Use, and lets Meta determine your location to decide where it applies. Meta describes this as limiting how it uses the data in US states with applicable privacy laws.

US privacy opt-out. If you opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information or of targeted advertising in "Ad privacy choices" (see "Your choices"), the app stops sending Meta app events, even if you allowed tracking. The change applies as soon as you close the choices form; for the rest of that session Meta’s software may still download its settings from Meta (which means Meta receives your IP address and basic technical information such as the iOS version), and from the next launch it isn’t started at all.

We see only Meta’s ad reports, which tell us how our ads performed. We don’t receive your Advertising Identifier through Meta, and we don’t link Meta’s reports to your support emails or puzzle progress. Meta’s handling of this data is described in the Meta Privacy Policy.

Your choices

  • Ad privacy choices. Each time the app starts, Google’s consent tool checks which privacy choices apply where you are. In US states whose privacy laws Google’s tool supports, you can opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of your personal information and of targeted advertising. Open the app’s Settings and tap "Ad privacy choices"; the button appears when such choices are available for your location. Your choice is saved on your iPhone and applied each time the app starts. An opt-out also stops the app sending Meta app events. Opting out does not necessarily stop all advertising data collection; information may still be used to deliver and measure ads and to prevent fraud.
  • Tracking. You can change your answer at any time in the iPhone’s Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking (or Settings > Hidden Phrase). The app checks your answer whenever you open or return to it. If you turn tracking off, Meta app events stop from that point, and Meta’s software isn’t started the next time the app opens. If you turn it on later, Meta’s software starts when you next open or return to the app, unless you have opted out in "Ad privacy choices". Turning off "Allow Apps to Request to Track" stops all apps from asking.
  • Not watching ads. The game can be played and finished without ever watching an ad.

Contacting support

We use this information to answer your question, troubleshoot problems and handle privacy requests. Please do not send passwords or other sensitive information that is not needed for your request.

Retention and deletion

  • Local game data: progress and settings stay on your iPhone until you delete the app. We cannot remotely access, restore or delete your local progress.
  • Support emails: we delete support emails, including any attachments, within 12 months after the support request is resolved, except where we are legally required to keep them longer. In that case, we keep only what the law requires and delete it when the requirement ends.
  • Google’s advertising data: Google determines retention under its own policies. Deleting the app or opting out does not automatically erase information Google has already received. See Google’s retention information and Google’s privacy controls.
  • Meta’s data: Meta determines retention under the Meta Privacy Policy. Turning tracking off or deleting the app stops further app events but does not automatically erase information Meta has already received.

To request access, correction or deletion of information we hold, email berkeleynathanael@gmail.com with the subject “Hidden Phrase privacy request”. We may ask for information needed to verify the request. We will respond as required by applicable law and explain any limitation. We cannot directly delete Google’s or Meta’s independently held advertising records; their privacy policies linked above explain their available controls.

Sharing a result

If you tap "Share result", the iPhone’s share sheet opens with a short message such as "I solved Hidden Phrase level 12! Can you uncover the mystery?". Nothing is sent unless you choose where to share it, and the message does not include the puzzle’s phrase.

Children

Hidden Phrase is intended for people aged 13 and over and is designed primarily for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The app itself doesn’t ask anyone for personal information; see "Advertising" and "Measuring our ads on Facebook and Instagram" for what Google’s and Meta’s software collect. If you believe a child under 13 has provided personal information to us, contact us at berkeleynathanael@gmail.com so we can investigate and delete information we hold as appropriate.

Selling and sharing personal information

We do not sell your information for money. Information is processed by Google for advertising, by Meta for measuring our ads if you allow tracking, and by our email provider when you contact support, as described in this policy. We may also disclose information when required by law. Under some US state privacy laws, letting advertising partners such as Google and Meta collect data for advertising can count as a "sale" or "sharing" of personal information. You can opt out as described in "Your choices" ("Ad privacy choices" in Settings, where available for your location, and Apple’s tracking setting).

Security

The information the app saves stays in the app’s own storage on your iPhone, protected by iOS’s app sandbox and data protection. Data sent by Google’s and Meta’s software is encrypted in transit. No storage or transmission method can be guaranteed completely secure.

Swiparoo: Word Search

Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 26, 2026

In short: your puzzle progress stays on your device. Swiparoo has no accounts, no analytics of its own and no servers, and it doesn't track you. Swiparoo uses Google AdMob for optional rewarded video ads, and Google collects some information from your device to provide and measure those ads, as described below.

Who we are

Swiparoo: Word Search (“Swiparoo”) is a word puzzle game for iPhone and iPad made by Nathanael Berkeley. Questions about this policy: berkeleynathanael@gmail.com. You can open this policy at any time from the Privacy Policy link on the Gardens screen.

Information stored on your device

Swiparoo saves your game in a file on your device: your current puzzle, the words you've found, your hints, puzzles you're replaying, and a list of ad rewards already given. The ad-reward list is made of random codes created on your device, so the same ad can't be rewarded twice. Swiparoo never sends this file anywhere.

  • The file is included in your device's iCloud or computer backup, depending on your backup settings.
  • Progress isn't synced between devices, and there's no account.
  • Deleting Swiparoo deletes your saved progress from the device.

Home Screen widgets

To show your progress in Swiparoo's widgets, the app writes a short summary to storage on your device that only Swiparoo and its widgets can read. It contains the number of puzzles solved, your current level, puzzle and garden names, garden progress, and which picture to show. It contains no letters, answers or found words.

The widgets only read this summary. They have no ads, don't connect to the internet and don't collect any information.

Rewarded ads (Google AdMob)

Swiparoo shows an ad only when you choose Watch ad · Find 1 word. Watching is never required to play. These ads are provided by Google AdMob through Google's Mobile Ads software, which is built into the app.

So that an ad is ready when you want one, Swiparoo starts Google's software and requests an ad from Google when the app opens. This happens even if you never watch an ad.

Swiparoo requests ads with ads personalization turned off, so the ads you see aren't chosen based on your past behavior. Swiparoo also turns off Google's “publisher first-party ID”, an identifier Google can otherwise use to recognize a person across one developer's apps.

To show ads, limit how often you see the same ad, measure and report ad performance, and prevent fraud, Google's software may collect the following from your device, according to Google's disclosure for app developers:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location;
  • a device identifier (see below);
  • advertising data, such as the ads you've seen;
  • interactions, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • performance data and crash logs.

Google processes this information under its own policies. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the Google Privacy Policy. Swiparoo doesn't receive this information about you. The developer sees only overall ad reports from AdMob, such as how many ads were shown.

Advertising identifiers and tracking

Swiparoo doesn't track you. It doesn't link information from Swiparoo with information from other companies' apps, websites or offline sources for targeted advertising or advertising measurement, and it doesn't share your information with data brokers.

  • Swiparoo doesn't ask for permission to track you through Apple's App Tracking Transparency, so your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) isn't available to the app or to Google's software.
  • With the settings Swiparoo uses (no tracking permission, ads personalization off, publisher first-party ID off), Google has told us that its ad software doesn't link device identifiers collected in Swiparoo with other companies' data for targeted advertising or measurement.
  • Google's software may still use Apple's identifier for vendors, which is limited to apps from the same developer, or session-based identifiers, for frequency capping and fraud detection. Identifiers used for frequency capping and aggregated ad reporting are, according to Google, used locally or in a de-identified way.

Swiparoo doesn't sell your information, and doesn't share it with anyone except as described in this policy.

Your privacy choices

  • US state privacy rights: if you live in a US state with consumer privacy laws, you can opt out of the “sale” or “sharing” of your personal information for advertising. Tap Privacy choices on the Gardens screen and choose Don't sell or share my data. Your choice is stored on your device in a standard format that Google's ad software reads and applies, and you can change it at any time. The Privacy choices button appears where Google's consent tool indicates that such a choice applies.
  • Not watching ads: if you never tap the ad button, no ad is shown. An ad may still be requested in advance, as described above.
  • Google controls: My Ad Center lets you manage how Google personalizes ads across its services.
  • Deleting your data: delete Swiparoo to remove your progress and widget summary from the device. Swiparoo's developer holds no personal information about you. For information Google holds, see the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Questions or requests: email berkeleynathanael@gmail.com.

Children

Swiparoo is rated 4+ on the App Store, but it isn't designed specifically for children, and it doesn't knowingly collect personal information from children. Ads in Swiparoo are requested with personalization turned off. If you believe a child has provided personal information, please contact us.

Changes to this policy

If Swiparoo changes how it handles information, for example by adding new features or ad settings, we'll update this page and its effective date.
